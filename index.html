Problems We Help Clarify

When the operation is busy, but performance is hard to see

We help leadership find the operational issues that deserve attention first, quantify their impact where the data permits, and build a realistic path forward.

Inventory outpaces sales

Cash is tied up in slow-moving, excess, or obsolete stock without a clear action plan.

Warehouse flow breaks down

Receiving, putaway, picking, replenishment, or staging creates recurring delays.

KPIs do not guide decisions

Leadership has reports, but lacks a focused scorecard that shows what is improving or declining.

Tribal knowledge drives work

Critical processes depend on individual experience instead of clear, usable procedures.

Capacity feels constrained

Space, labor, material flow, or scheduling limits output, but the root cause is unclear.

Data is available but unused

The team has operational data but lacks the time or structure to turn it into action.

Our Approach

Practical analysis, not generic recommendations

The goal is not to produce a longer report. It is to make the operational problem clearer, show which opportunities matter, and create a plan the organization can actually use.

Use the operation's own dataRecommendations are grounded in the client's processes, constraints, and information.
Quantify impact where possibleFinancial and operational estimates are clearly labeled and tied to available data.
Prioritize effort and valueQuick wins and longer-term improvements are separated so the team knows where to start.
Remain independentWe do not sell software, freight capacity, or equipment, so the analysis is focused on fit.

Focused Assessment

Independent Truck vs. Inland Port Review

For manufacturers comparing a Norfolk Southern or inland-port proposal with an OTR carrier offer or counteroffer.

  • Apples-to-apples proposal comparison
  • Annual and per-container cost model
  • Inventory and working-capital impact
  • Service and operational-risk scorecard
  • Break-even and sensitivity analysis
  • Recommendation, pilot options, and success criteria

The conclusion may support staying with OTR, converting selected freight to rail, negotiating better terms, or testing the change through a controlled pilot.
